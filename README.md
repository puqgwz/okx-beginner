## How to use OKX spot trading

Spot trading is where many beginners become confused because the screen contains a chart, order book, trades, balances, and several order types at once. You do not need to use all of them immediately.

### Choose a trading pair

A trading pair tells you what you are buying and what you are paying with.

- In **BTC/USDT**, BTC is the asset being bought or sold.
- USDT is the quote asset used to price BTC.
- A buy order uses the quote asset to acquire the base asset.
- A sell order exchanges the base asset for the quote asset.

Choose a pair with sufficient liquidity and confirm that it is available in your jurisdiction.

### Market order

A market order executes at the best available prices in the order book. It is straightforward, but the final execution price can differ from the price you saw a moment earlier, particularly in a thin market or during a sharp move.

Market orders are convenient when execution matters more than exact price control.

### Limit order

A limit order lets you specify the price at which you are willing to buy or sell. It may fill immediately, partially, later, or not at all.

For example, if BTC is trading at $60,000 and you place a buy limit order at $59,500, the order will generally wait for the market to reach your chosen price. A limit order gives you price control, but it does not guarantee execution.

OKX identifies maker and taker fees based on how an order interacts with the order book. An order that executes immediately against existing orders is treated as a taker order; an order that adds liquidity may be treated as a maker order.

### A sensible first spot trade

For a first transaction, use a small amount and record:

- The trading pair.
- The order type.
- The amount submitted.
- The estimated fee.
- The final filled amount.
- The average execution price.

This gives you a practical understanding of the interface without turning a tutorial into an expensive experiment.

- Where do the rewards come from?
- Can the asset price fall while you earn?
- Is the product using a third-party protocol?
- Is there smart-contract or validator risk?
- Is the displayed rate variable?

## Common beginner mistakes on OKX

### Using futures before understanding spot trading

Futures add leverage, margin, funding costs, and liquidation risk. If you are still asking what a limit order does, futures are probably not the next logical button.

### Choosing the wrong network

The asset name is not enough. BTC, USDT, ETH, and other assets may be available across multiple networks. The network selected at OKX must match the network used by the sending or receiving wallet.

### Ignoring the final quote

A displayed asset price is not the same as the final execution price. Review the order preview, trading fee, spread, and received amount.

### Sending everything at once

Use a test transfer for a new wallet or unfamiliar network. A small test is cheaper than discovering that the address or chain was wrong after sending the full balance.

### Assuming every feature is available

OKX services vary by region. A feature visible in a global help article may not be available in your account.

### Treating promotional rewards as guaranteed cash

Referral and campaign terms can include trading requirements, time limits, geographic restrictions, account-status rules, or reward caps. Read the conditions shown in your own account before counting a promotion as part of your balance.
